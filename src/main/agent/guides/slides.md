# Slides

Frame: each slide is its own top-level frame, 1920 x 1080 fixed, layout "vertical", padding 120. Place slides in a row with 80 between them, named "01 / Title", "02 / Problem" and so on.

Principles:
- One message per slide. The title states the takeaway ("Churn fell 18% after onboarding v2"), not the topic ("Churn").
- Slides support a speaker; they are not documents. Short phrases, no paragraphs, at most five bullets.
- If content does not fit at the sizes below, split the slide or cut content. Never shrink the type.
- Consistency over novelty: same title position, margins and colours on every content slide.

Type: titles 56-72, body 32-40, labels 24, big numbers 160-240. At most two families. Line height 1.1-1.2 for titles, 1.3 for body. High contrast always.

Colour: two or three brand colours plus neutrals. The accent marks the one thing to look at.

Layouts to choose from:
- Cover: title, subtitle, presenter and date; large and calm.
- Section break: small label plus a big title, nothing else.
- Statement: one sentence at 72-96, optionally an attribution.
- Big number: label, the number as the hero, one line of context.
- Two or three numbers side by side with equal weight.
- Text + visual: title and up to four short lines on one half, image or diagram on the other half.
- Three pillars: three columns, each a visual, a short label and one line.
- Compare: two columns with matching structure, or before/after with the "after" side emphasised.
- Process: three to five steps in a row with numbers and one-line descriptions.
- Chart + insight: chart takes most of the slide, one bold sentence says what it means, one highlighted data point.
- Quote: the quote large, name and role small.
- Closing: one memorable line plus contact or next step.

Opening and closing slides should feel like statements: strong image or bold type, little text.

Charts: build bars from rectangles inside layout frames so labels align automatically. One insight per chart; mute everything except the highlighted bar.
